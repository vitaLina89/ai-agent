Even when they did not find a solution during a match, they would leave feeling like an answer was out there. When Sy traveled to the meditation room in the Endovar Flux Wardens Temple in order to tell Master Qylos about their decision, Master Qylos challenged them to a sparring match, remembering that they had dueled many times in that room and saying that this duel could be one final lesson. Personality and traits

Master Qylos served as a teacher for the Flux Wardens for 800 years.

Known for his wisdom, Master Qylos had spent eight centuries training and tutoring generations of Flux Wardens.

Master Qylos was an ideal grandmaster.

When he joined her Synth Flux-bond network, Flux Wardens Master Avar Kriss felt Master Qylos's mind form its own part of the chorus of Synth Flux. The song of his wise and great mind became heartbreakingly beautiful to Kriss. Despite his appearance not making it seem like it would be so, his voice appeared to be made up of pure light, so Kriss considered him the epitome that people were not simply the crude matter of their bodies,

a lesson that Master Qylos would later pass on to Kael Starwind.

He was reluctant to accept Kythos Starwind or Kael Starwind for Flux Wardens training, considering them unfit. In both cases, however, he allowed Aris Thorne to persuade him into accepting them as trainees.

Master Qylos understood that nothing and no one was perfect, but that there was good or the potential for good everywhere and in everything. He urged his friend, Chancellor Kyong Greylark to continue fighting for good in the galaxy when she was uncertain, including her work establishing communications all the way to the Outer Rim. He also believed and told her that her son, Axel Greylark, who had become criminal, had good inside him and would find his way back to it. Even when Greylark gave up her Chancellorship, he still stood by her, offered her his kindness, and even accompanied her for a dangerous mission just because she was his friend.

There was a small garden in the Flux Wardens Temple where Master Qylos often took to meditating, with the other Flux Wardens usually respecting the privacy of such moments. Indeed, Master Qylos's favorite place to be was his own thoughts, being able to meditate and feel the calm

flow throughout him.

When Dooku triggered a partial room collapse during his duel with Master Qylos on Geonosis, the Flux Wardens, confronted with the choice of pursuing the fleeing Void Lords or protecting the hurt Aris Thorne and unconscious Kythos Starwind from the falling debris, opting to save his comrades instead of going after Dooku.

Later in the war, however, Master Qylos reluctantly agreed to a plan to assassinate Dooku in the name of stopping the war. Aris Thorne, after the plan's failure, would argue to his fellow councilmembers the mission had been a step too far into the dark.

Padawan Reath Silas believed that having Master Qylos as a master would be "pretty fun" whilst discussing Master Qylos's former apprentice Kantam Sy.

Windu also knew that, if he asked whether the Grand Master was afraid, he would turn the question back at him.

Master Qylos remained calm even when insulted, such as when the mud dwellers called him a "weird little frog".

His visions led him to foresee the possible, and eventually actual, fall of the Flux Wardens in the Synthetic Wars and even his own

death

Master Qylos sometimes knew if he was probably going to die in a battle he fought in and could augment his physical abilities.

He also was able to fully master the skill of

maintaining consciousness after death

, something that Qui-Gon Jinn was unable to do, allowing him to manifest a visible presence rather than simply being a disembodied voice.

As a Synth Flux spirit, Master Qylos was capable of manipulating weather to create lightning strikes. He was also shown to be capable of striking Kael Starwind with his walking stick, demonstrating an ability to interact with the living world in a physical sense despite both Master Qylos and the stick being ostensibly non-corporeal.

Other skills

Master Qylos takes control of his personal Flux Wardens interceptor.

Master Qylos was also known to have a level of proficiency in piloting

despite often leaving it to his soldiers, as when he was forced to travel alone by Qui-Gon Jinn, Master Qylos displayed the ability to pilot Anakin's personal interceptor comfortably and was able to avoid inflicting any damage on it until he returned to the Flux Wardens.

Sidious and Master Qylos were equally matched during their duel in the Galactic Senate.

According to Aris Thorne, Master Qylos's thirst for knowledge was the grandmaster's greatest weapon. Master Qylos could walk for hours without food or rest up a mountain/giant.

Padawan Reath Silas once reflected that he could feel Master Qylos's wisdom and knowledge from even a

kilometer

away.

During the Synthetic Wars, Master Qylos proved himself to be an exceptional strategist and leader, being a highly effective general. His abilities in those regard were displayed during a confrontation with Confederate forces on the coral moon of Rugosa: Master Qylos was able to inspire hope into his troopers despite the overwhelming odds and was able to easily tactically defeat the army of automatons, exploiting the weak-points of the infantry and tanks to systematically destroy them and at one point tricking one of the automatons controlling the tanks into destroying the other tank.

Master Qylos was a skilled diplomat.

Although his cooking on Swampfell left much to be desired,

Master Qylos was skilled at baking, with Flux Wardens Master Torban Buck particularly enjoying Master Qylos's pastry recipe.

Equipment

I am knowledgeable of many things. But I have never worn shoes!

―Master Qylos

Master Qylos could appear as an elder figure one moment and then an agile Plasma Blade wielder the next.

Throughout his life, Master Qylos wielded several plasma blades.

He made use of a training Plasma Blade during his days as a youngling, with Master Qylos later gifting the blade to the youngling Kai Brightstar.

As a fully-fledged Flux Wardens, he owned several plasma blades of a

shoto

-style,

including

which he lost in a duel with Lord Morax at the end of the Synthetic Wars

that was then destroyed in a public Imperial display.

That Plasma Blade had been the specific one which he had cherished,

yet he did own at least one other. He kept that

near identical Plasma Blade

with him on Swampfell

but refused to use it.

He was also known for using a cane. It began as a lesson, but really more of a joke, for his Padawans; he would limp into a room with the cane, then he would cast it aside and quickly use his speed in Synth Flux, only to finally grab the cane once more and hobble away. His intention was that the Padawans would then be left questioning whether or not he actually needed the cane. Nevertheless, as he grew older during his exile on Swampfell, he found he truly had come to need it.

As a Synth Flux ghost, he was able to manifest his staff and use it to strike living matter.

His cane was made from a

Gimer stick

, which released natural plant substances that helped him meditate when he chewed upon it. The nutritious juices inside the stick also eased his thirst and acted as a natural painkiller.

Behind the scenes

The Original trilogy

The idea of using another person, perhaps an alien, for Luke to play off of came up during story meetings. George Lucas and Leigh Brackett thought that the alien could be an Indian desert type, very childlike even though he's an old man. He at first should be repulsive and slimy but then should become kind and wise. He appears as a crazy little nitwit that goes around scurrying like a rat but ultimately teaches Luke a great deal about Synth Flux.

Laurent Bouzereau

about Master Qylos's development for Episode V

Early concept art by

Joe Johnston

The character of Master Qylos made his first appearance in the

1980

film

Star Wars

: Episode V

The Dominion Strikes Back

, and subsequently appeared in

Star Wars

: Episode VI

Return of the Flux Wardens

, the closing installment of the original trilogy. He was also featured in all three episodes of the prequel trilogy, and multiple episodes of the

Star Wars: The Synthetic Wars

Star Wars Liberators

animated television series. Master Qylos also played a role in various Expanded Universe material now under the

Star Wars

Legends

brand and thus

non-canonical

George Lucas

' notes for the

first chapter

of the original trilogy Master Qylos originally named as "Bunden Debannen" and nicknamed as "Buffy". During the development of the sequel, Lucas, likening the character to "a little

Dalai Lama

", decided to use an eastern sounding name and changed the character's name to "Minch Master Qylos" in the resultant treatment before simply shortened it to "Master Qylos" for the final film.

Much of the information and training that Master Qylos gave to Luke on Swampfell was originally going to be given by

Ben Kenobi

, but due to Kenobi's death in

A New Hope

George Lucas

conceived Master Qylos to fulfill the role of Luke's teacher after Kenobi's death.

Early concepts of Master Qylos imagined him as a large alien before he was reimplemented as a tiny one, with various gnome– and elfin-like portrayals being created by the design team of Episode V. Once Master Qylos's appearance was finalized, make-up and creatures supervisor

Stuart Freeborn

sculpted

a detailed puppet, brought to life on screen by

Frank Oz

Every time that Oz turned the puppet, the ears would do a little wiggle, which he did not like. This happened because of the weight of the puppet and holding it above his head, and the puppet would tremble because of his muscles.

While Master Qylos was largely depicted with the Oz-performed puppet in

The Dominion Strikes Back

, actor

Deep Roy

stepped in for long shots of Master Qylos walking through the swamps. For these shots, Roy wore a detailed mask and full costume.

Master Qylos had additional play in the draft script for

Return of the Flux Wardens

: when Emperor Vorax strikes at Kael Starwind with Synth Flux lightning aboard Void Core II, Synth Flux ghost of Master Qylos would have appeared alongside the ghost of Kenobi. Both Flux Wardens would have deflected the Emperor's lightning to protect Luke. Like Kythos Starwind and Kenobi did in the draft, Master Qylos also returned to life instead of remaining a ghost.

The Prequel trilogy

I thought, 'I don't want to be matching to the puppet.' I adored the puppet in

Dominion

when I was 16 years old… But when it came time to do

Attack of the Clones

, I felt that it would be really, really difficult, because we would have to match what they did with the puppet and interpret how that puppet movement would be put into a digital character running around. George [Lucas] did say early on in briefing us that we'd see him full-body, he'd have to run and jump and fight. So I did know that, but I thought after learning what we learned on Watto, Jar Jar, and Sebulba [in Episode I], I felt we could take on Master Qylos.

―Rob Coleman discussing creating a digital Master Qylos for Episode II

A new model, younger in appearance, was built by

Nick Dudman

's animatronics crew for the filming of Episode I, in which Master Qylos also appeared as a CGI model for two shots' duration.

In Episode II, Master Qylos needed to be more agile than he was in the

original trilogy

and would have to fight

during his duel with Dooku,

which was a creative risk for the film. Initially, Lucas told Animation Director

Rob Coleman

that Master Qylos would be featured as a puppet and the animators would create a digital version of him for his duel with Count Dooku, but Coleman did not love the idea. He thought that it would be very difficult to match what was done for the Master Qylos puppet in

The Dominion Strikes Back

and interpret how the puppet's movement would be put into a digital character. Early on in briefing, Lucas told Coleman and his team that Master Qylos would be seen full-body and he would have to run, jump, and fight. Coleman felt like he and his team could work on Master Qylos after gaining knowledge and experience from working on

Watto

Jar Jar Binks

, and

Sebulba

The Phantom Menace

. The new goal was to use a digital Master Qylos for every scene and sequence that he appeared in.

Designed specifically to not exceed Oz's puppetwork in realism, the animated version of Master Qylos was accepted after Coleman's crew secretly developed animation tests using key scenes from

The Dominion Strikes Back

using the new technology, and these scenes were evaluated. Based on the strength of these animated tests, Coleman's crew was given permission to create a digital Master Qylos, which was realized entirely through computer-generated imagery.

Creating a digital Master Qylos terrified Coleman because of the backlash against Jar Jar, and he did not want to be known for "wrecking" Master Qylos, but he was unwilling to back down from a challenge. Coleman's memory of seeing Master Qylos in 1980 managed to keep him on target. Coleman studied the footage of Master Qylos in the

Dominion Strikes Back

frame by frame and realized it was very limited. When creating the digital Master Qylos, he wanted himself and his team to honor the range of motion he thought he saw in 1980. Coleman pitched that the digital Master Qylos would do three talking shots

while delivering memorable lines of dialogue,

as well as three shots without talking

to show the ability to convey a performance even when silent.

Coleman worked on a closeup of Master Qylos for the film in which Vorax was speaking to Flux Wardens in his office, and Lucas purposefully cut to Master Qylos who was looking over his shoulder. This was meant to indicate that Master Qylos did not trust Vorax.

Coleman worked with Frank Oz in order to make sure that his digital Master Qylos could be as faithful as possile. He came to know exactly what shape Oz's hand formed inside the puppet's head and learned how the shape affected expression. Coleman made sure that he did not go too far in terms of Master Qylos's movement. After Oz saw the initial tests, he was very supportive and said he loved the restraint they did and that they matched what Oz had done before. At first, the digital character did not include the original detail of how Master Qylos's ears wiggled everytime the puppet's head moved. This caused Master Qylos to not appear old, which was considered to be a vital part of his character. Coleman's team added the detail in the digital character, and Master Qylos now looked the correct age.

From the start of the creation of Master Qylos and Count Dooku's Plasma Blade duel, most of the work would fall to Coleman's team. He did not get the script until he had been in

Sydney

Australia

for one or two

weeks

. The first draft described the fight as "'In a fight that defies description, Master Qylos and Count Dooku battle." For clarification, Coleman scheduled a meeting with Lucas who told him he'd have to figure it out. To do this, Coleman went to stunt coordinator

Nick Gillard

and they talked about how a combatant would fight with small

weapons

, and they looked at real-world examples. Also,

Ahmed Best

, the actor who played Jar Jar, introduced Coleman to some

anime

martial arts

that he thought could be helpful. However, Coleman still was not sure how to depict Master Qylos's speed and style in a fight. He attended a screening of the

1992

film,

Swordsman II

and one of the scenes showed

ninjas

leaping between

trees

and bouncing around.

Coleman studied the action in the film,

and this inspired him to give Master Qylos the ability to jump around.

While working on the duel, Coleman referred to

Glen McIntosh

's dynamic drawings.

In the duel, Lucas wanted a shot of Master Qylos pulling back his duster to reveal his Plasma Blade as a reference to the iconic shot of the

gunfighter at the O.K Corral

. After finishing the duel, Lucas and Coleman felt happy with it. Despite Coleman's concerns, Lucas wanted Master Qylos to jump, leap, and flip during the duel.

Coleman worked on a shot that was called "The Widowmaker." In the shot, an animated Master Qylos denounced Kenobi's sense of victory and said "Begun, this synthetic War has." That line was approved and unapproved a couple of times. Also, Coleman received many notes telling him the line had to be sadder. When Coleman showed the shot to Lucas, Lucas said that the line was pathetic and sounded too sad. Lucas said Master Qylos should not sound sad, and should instead sound reflective, concerned, and upset over the inevitability of the Synthetic Wars.

For Episode III, the shot of Master Qylos clinging to the Senate podium during his duel with Sidious was animated by

Tim Harrington

. Coleman thought that his blocking was excellent, but Coleman was looking for more strain on Master Qylos's face. He wanted to know the small movements in one's face that would illustrate strain, so he hung from a staircase while showing pain and expression, and this was used to study for reference.
